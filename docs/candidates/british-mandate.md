## Candidates

### 1. Balfour Declaration, 2 November 1917
- Date: letter dated 2 Nov 1917 (Arthur Balfour to Lord Rothschild); War Cabinet approved release 31 Oct; published 9 Nov [W].
- Location: London; effect Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: political. Era: use `british-mandate` (boundary year 1917; owner to confirm).
- Why: First statement by a great power favouring "a national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine, with a safeguard for the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities.
- **[DISPUTED]** Zionist view: first major-power recognition. Arab view (Sharif Hussein, Muslim-Christian Association petition of 3 Nov 1918, King-Crane 1919): breach of the McMahon-Hussein correspondence and of self-determination. Scholars: Said, Shlaim, Hourani. Authorship and motives debated (propaganda aims in Russia and the US were seen only in an unread snippet). **[2ND]** Palestinian primary sources not yet read.
- Read: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/balfour.asp (text); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration (partial) [W]. Britannica 403.

### 2. San Remo conference (April 1920) and confirmation of the Mandate for Palestine (1922-23)
- Date: San Remo 19-26 Apr 1920 (resolution of 25 Apr); League of Nations Council approval 22 July 1922, text dated 24 July 1922; in force 29 Sept 1923 [W]. **[DATE]** 22 vs 24 July (Avalon text is dated 24 July; Wikipedia says approved 22nd). Check League Official Journal.
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`; venues San Remo, London, Geneva in summary. Category: political.
- Why: Gave Britain the Mandate, incorporating the Balfour Declaration, and set the legal framework for 1923-1948.
- Read: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_Century/Palmanda.asp (text); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Remo_conference [W]; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine_(legal_instrument) [W]. Contested legal interpretation not yet researched **[2ND]**.

### 3. Nebi Musa disturbances, Jerusalem, 4-7 April 1920
- Date: 4-7 April 1920 (Churchill in Commons: began 4 Apr, sporadic attacks to 8 Apr). Martial law 5 Apr [W].
- Location: Jerusalem (Old City). Category: conflict.
- **[DISPUTED]** Casualties: Churchill (Hansard, 29 Apr 1920) about 250 injured or killed, about nine-tenths Jewish; Segev (2001, via [W]): 5 Jews and 4 Arabs killed, 216 Jews and 23 Arabs wounded; a military inquiry quoted in the Survey of Palestine (via [W]): 211 Jews and 21 Arabs injured. Palin Court of Inquiry (50 days, 152 witnesses, not published at the time): no general bias by the Administration; Zionist Commission overstepped its role. Arab accounts frame it as political protest; Zionist accounts as a pogrom with alleged British complicity (Weizmann); Meinertzhagen's allegations are "never substantiated" per [W].
- Read: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1920/apr/29/statement-by-mr-churchill ; https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/yabber/yabber_palin.html (partial transcription of FO 371/5121); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Nebi_Musa_riots [W].

### 4. Jaffa riots, 1-7 May 1921
- Date: began 1 May 1921 (clash between rival Jewish May Day marches), resumed 3 May, calm by about 4 May; raids on Petah Tikva, Hadera, Rehovot, Kfar Saba [W]. Commission appointed 7 May, report 10 Aug 1921.
- Location: Jaffa and Jewish colonies nearby. Category: conflict.
- **[DISPUTED]** Early telegram (Hansard Lords, 8 June 1921): 10 Arabs and 30 Jews killed; final figures reported higher. Haycraft report, via [W]: 47 Jews and 48 Arabs killed, 146 Jews and 73 Arabs wounded (table itself unread). Haycraft findings (JVL résumé): root cause Arab hostility tied to immigration and Zionist policy; Arabs generally the aggressors; trigger a Jewish Communist demonstration; police half-trained and in places complicit. Many Arab deaths were from military and police action.
- Read: https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1921/jun/08/situation-in-palestine ; https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/haycraft-commission-of-inquiry-into-the-1920-21-arab-riots-october-1921 (JVL title says Oct 1921, report dated 10 Aug) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_riots_(May_1921) [W]. **[2ND]** Arab-perspective source needed.

### 5. 1922 White Paper and the 1922 census
- Date: White Paper June 1922 (Colonial Office statement; Churchill was Colonial Secretary); census 23 Oct 1922.
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: political / demographic.
- Why: The White Paper said the national home would be founded "in" Palestine, not make Palestine as a whole Jewish, and tied immigration to economic absorptive capacity. The census is the first official count: total 757,182 (Muslims 590,890; Jews 83,794; Christians 73,024; Druze 7,028) per Barron, *Census of 1922* (via [W]).
- **[DISPUTED]** The Arab delegation disputed the White Paper's reading of the McMahon letter (historiographic account, not shown on the page read). Census caveats: Southern District Bedouin estimated (about 72,898), some northern villages and Transjordan not counted; Hebrew Wikipedia gave slightly different Muslim and Jewish totals, probably typos.
- Read: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brwh1922.asp (text); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine [W]. Consider splitting into two entries.

### 6. Supreme Muslim Council founded; Haj Amin al-Husayni becomes Mufti of Jerusalem (1921-22)
- Date: SMC created by order of High Commissioner Samuel, December 1921; first election 9 Jan 1922; Husayni appointed Mufti 1921 or 1922 **[DATE]** (sources differ).
- Location: Jerusalem. Category: political / religious-sites.
- Why: Gave the Muslim community a body to run waqf (endowment) and sharia affairs under Husayni's presidency; dissolved by the British in 1937.
- **[DISPUTED]** Husayni: supporters see an early advocate of independence; critics call him a sponsor of violence and wartime collaborator with Nazi Germany; a middle view says early cooperation with the British weakened Arab influence. The account that he did not top the electoral list is NOT confirmed.
- Read: https://encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/supreme-muslim-council ; https://encyclopedia.com/politics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/al-husayni-al-hajj-amin . Britannica 403. **[2ND]** needs a scholarly source (Kupferschmidt on the SMC).

### 7. The 1929 disturbances (Western Wall dispute; Hebron and Safed), 23-29 August 1929
- Date: Wall demonstrations 14-16 Aug; violence from 23 Aug; Hebron 24 Aug; Safed 29 Aug.
- Location: Jerusalem; Hebron; Safed. Category: conflict. (`approximate` for Palestine-wide, or split by city.)
- **[DISPUTED]** Figures cited to the Shaw Report p.65 (via [W], report unread): Jews 133 killed, wounded 198 (Shaw) to 339 in other sources; Arabs 116 killed, at least 232 wounded, mostly by police and army. Hebron: about 65-68 Jews killed (Encyclopedia.com: 67); Safed 18-20. 174 Arabs and 109 Jews charged with murder or attempted murder; 2 Jews and 17 Arabs sentenced to death, nearly all Arab sentences commuted (Encyclopedia.com). Shaw Commission (report March 1930): cause was Arab animosity linked to disappointed national hopes and fear for livelihood and land; Jewish demonstration of 15 Aug the main trigger; not premeditated; no established link to the Mufti (Snell dissented). Historians differ on rumours about al-Aqsa (Shapira, Pappé) versus land and economic anxiety (Winder, Cohen). Hundreds of Hebron Jews were sheltered by Arab neighbours; Jewish leaders declined Haganah protection. Handle Hebron with great care.
- Read: https://www.encyclopedia.com/politics/energy-government-and-defense-magazines/hebron-massacre ; https://encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/shaw-commission ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Palestine_riots (partial) [W]. PalQuest copy of the Shaw report 403.

### 8. Hope Simpson report and Passfield White Paper, October 1930
- Date: Oct 1930. **[DATE]** Hope Simpson report dated 30 Aug (Encyclopedia.com) or 1 Oct and released 21 Oct (Wikipedia). Passfield White Paper issued alongside; MacDonald's Feb 1931 letter to Weizmann in effect reversed it ("Black Letter" to Arabs).
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: land-settlement / political.
- Why: Found little cultivable land left for new farming immigrants and urged limiting immigration to economic capacity; reported Arab landlessness and unemployment.
- **[DISPUTED]** The Jewish Agency disputed the statistical basis in 1931 (title seen only). Figures like "30% landless" are hedged in the source and are not safe. Wadi al-Hawarith (JNF purchase from absentee owners, tenant eviction, court order Sept 1930) is from snippets only and needs the IJMES article "The tenants of Wadi Hawarith" read.
- Read: https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/hope-simpson-commission-1930 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Simpson_Enquiry [W]. Avalon copy 404. **[2ND]**.

### 9. Death of Sheikh Izz ad-Din al-Qassam, 20 November 1935
- Date: 20 Nov 1935 [W]; Encyclopedia.com says 19 Nov **[DATE]**. Place: cave near Ya'bad, Jenin sub-district (Wikipedia also names Sheikh Zeid) **[LOC]**.
- Category: conflict. Context: born in Jableh, Syria; preacher in Haifa; organised secret cells from about 1930; went to the hills after a police sergeant was found dead in Nov 1935; he and 3 followers were killed.
- **[DISPUTED]** Interpretations (via [W]): Khalidi, a shift from elite compromise to armed struggle; Schleifer, his death "electrified" Palestinians; Burke, a social reformer angered by peasant dispossession; Milton-Edwards, Islamic politics opposed by the urban elite. The Qassam Brigades of Hamas later took his name (Encyclopedia.com) - keep that out of the card or attribute it.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_ad-Din_al-Qassam [W]; https://www.encyclopedia.com/politics/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/qassam-izz-al-din-al . **[2ND]**.

### 10. The Arab Revolt, 1936-1939
- Date: killings near Tulkarm 15 Apr 1936; Arab Higher Committee formed 25 Apr 1936; general strike about six months (19 Apr and 12 Oct are commonly cited but NOT in pages read, **[DATE]**); second phase from Sept 1937; suppressed 1939.
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: conflict.
- **[DISPUTED]** Arab deaths: about 5,000 (Khalidi, via Encyclopedia.com; OSU); 5,032 including 3,832 killed by the British and 1,200 in intra-Arab violence (Khalidi's reading of British statistics via Hughes 2019 and [W]); British official figures give over 2,000 in combat, plus 108 hanged and 961 killed in "gang and terrorist activities" (Hughes). Executions: 112 (Khalidi) vs 108 (Levenberg). Jewish dead: about 415 (OSU) to about 500 (Levenberg via [W]); British dead 100 (OSU) vs 262 (Levenberg) - sources conflict. Intra-Arab assassinations (Nashashibi-Husayni rivalry) also occurred. Interpretations: unity and defiance (OSU) versus a defeat that weakened Palestinian capacity in 1948 (Khalidi, OSU); Yishuv became more self-reliant.
- Read: https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/palestine-arab-revolt-1936-1939 ; https://origins.osu.edu/read/1936-1939-arab-revolt-palestine ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine (first 100k of 305k) [W]. Britannica 403. Hughes, *Britain's Pacification of Palestine* (2019) is the book to check.

### 11. Peel Commission report and partition proposal, July 1937
- Date: July 1937 (7 July per owner brief; the JVL page says only July **[DATE]**).
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: political.
- Why: First official British proposal to partition: a Jewish state of about 17% (coast, Jezreel Valley, Galilee), an Arab state of about 75% joined to Transjordan, about 8% (Jaffa-Jerusalem corridor) under British mandate, with land exchange and "transfer of population" (JVL).
- **[DISPUTED]** The Arab Higher Committee rejected partition (formally 11 Oct 1938); the Bludan congress (Sept 1937) is a snippet only. The 20th Zionist Congress (Zurich, Aug 1937) rejected the specific plan but authorised negotiation; Morris is paraphrased as saying leaders saw it as a stepping stone while others say they accepted partition in principle - unread. Whether transfer was meant as compulsory is debated; the 200,000+ figure is unverified. The primary PDF (israeled.org copy) was not opened. **[2ND]**.
- Read: https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/the-peel-commission .

### 12. The White Paper of May 1939
- Date: 17 May 1939 per brief, Cmd. 6019 (not shown on Avalon); JVL says 23 May 1939 **[DATE]** (possibly the Commons debate; unverified).
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: political.
- Why: Proposed an independent Palestine within 10 years, about 75,000 Jewish immigrants over five years then Arab consent, and limits on land transfers. Quota detail differs: Avalon summary (10,000 a year plus 25,000 refugees) vs a US consular despatch (50,000 plus 25,000 refugees) - check the text. The paper put the Jewish population at about 450,000, nearly a third. First quota 15 June 1939; the Oct 1939-Mar 1940 quota suspended (FRUS). About 51,000 of 75,000 certificates used by 1944 (snippet only).
- **[DISPUTED]** The Jewish Agency called it a denial of Jewish rights. Arab Higher Committee, Mufti and Permanent Mandates Commission reactions NOT found.
- Read: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/brwh1939.asp ; https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/the-british-white-papers ; https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1940v03/d767 . **[2ND]**.

### 13. Haifa harbour opens (1933) and the Mandate economy
- Date: harbour opened October 1933 (31 Oct per Chicago Daily Tribune via [W]) **[DATE]** Wikipedia gives construction start 1922 vs 1929 elsewhere. Iraq Petroleum pipeline: 1931 agreement; completion 1934 vs official opening 14 Jan 1935 **[DATE]**.
- Location: Haifa. Category: economy-daily-life.
- Why: Deepwater port and the oil pipeline terminus made Haifa a hub. Economic divergence: 1922-1947 the Jewish sector grew 13.2% a year versus 6.5% for the Arab sector, and by 1936 Jewish-sector income was 2.6 times the Arab sector's (Khalidi 2006 pp.13-14, via [W], unverified). Electricity: Rutenberg's Jaffa Electric Company, 1923; wider Palestine Electric Corporation dates NOT found.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Mandatory_Palestine [W]; http://wondersofworldengineering.com/palestines_harbours.html (weak); https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1933/feb/15/iraq-oil-pipe-lines . Weakly sourced; may need replacing.

### 14. Palestine's citrus industry under the Mandate
- Date: 1920s-1948 (`century`/range); export peak 1938-39 (figure unverified).
- Location: Jaffa region, `approximate`. Category: economy-daily-life.
- Why: Citrus was the leading export and had Arab and Jewish growers. Kabha and Karlinsky (*The Lost Orchard*, Syracuse UP, 2021, via a Jadaliyya review): Arab citriculture predated Zionist immigration; a bi-national growers' organisation formed about 1939-40 and ran to April 1948; about 3,800 Palestinian growers became refugees in 1948 (Israeli 1948-49 census). JTA (1932): about 150,000 dunams and 3.5 million boxes exported a year, Jewish ownership at least half, unattributed.
- **[DISPUTED]** Which community held the larger share differs by source. Export totals (15.2 million boxes in 1938-39 was a UN snippet, unread) and wartime effects NOT confirmed.
- Read: https://www.jadaliyya.com/Details/44451 ; JTA 27 July 1932. Weak - consider cutting or restricting to the Kabha-Karlinsky claims.

### 15. Jewish immigration and population in the 1930s
- Date: 1929/1932-1939 (Fifth Aliyah). **[DATE]** JVL says 1929-1939.
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: demographic.
- Why: Legal immigration (US consular estimates of official data): 1935 61,854 (peak); 1936 29,727; 1937 10,536; 1938 12,868; 1939 about 16,000-16,500 plus an estimated 14,000-15,000 illegal. 1932-34 figures conflict across snippets. Totals for the Fifth Aliyah vary: nearly 250,000 by 1940 (JVL), about 200,000 (bpb.de), 225,000-300,000 ([W]). Census: 1922 total 757,182; 1931 total 1,035,821 (Muslims 759,712; Jews 174,610; Christians 91,398) per JVL mirroring the census reports. Survey of Palestine 1946 (snippet only, unread): total 1,764,520; Muslims 1,061,270; Jews 553,600; Christians 135,550.
- **[DISPUTED]** Arab opposition to immigration was a main driver of the 1936 strike; scale and effects debated. The immigration quota system dated from 1937 after economic absorptive-capacity schedules.
- Read: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1940v03/d767 ; https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Immigration/Fifth_Aliyah.html ; https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/british-census-of-palestine-1931 . Could be merged with 5.

### 16. King David Hotel bombing, Jerusalem, 22 July 1946
- Date: 22 July 1946, about 12:37 pm.
- Location: King David Hotel, Jerusalem (south wing held the Secretariat and military offices). Category: conflict.
- Why: Irgun bomb killed 91 people (92 on a 2006 plaque), 45-49 injured (sources differ). Dead by group [W citing Hoffman 2020]: 41 Arabs, 28 British, 17 Jews, 2 Armenians, 1 Russian, 1 Greek, 1 Egyptian.
- **[DISPUTED]** Irgun/Begin: three phone warnings about 25 minutes before the blast, ignored by a British official. British inquest: no warning reached anyone with power to act; Chief Secretary John Shaw denied receiving one; the police report said the Consulate call came after the blast (Clarke disputes this). Jewish Agency and Jewish National Council condemned the attack; a 1979 MP testimony alleged an officer joked about a threat (JVL).
- Read: https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/History/King_David.html (Irgun-sympathetic framing) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_David_Hotel_bombing [W]. Hoffman PDF unreadable. **[2ND]** needs a British primary source (Hansard 24 July 1946 not found).

### 17. UN General Assembly Resolution 181 (Partition Plan), 29 November 1947
- Date: 29 Nov 1947. Vote 33 for, 13 against, 10 abstentions, 1 absent [W].
- Location: New York (decision); effect Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: political.
- Why: Recommended ending the Mandate and creating an Arab state, a Jewish state and an international Jerusalem zone with economic union. Shares [W, unsourced]: Arab state about 42.9%, Jewish state about 56.5%, Jerusalem zone about 0.65%; 1946 population about 1,203,000 Arabs, 608,000 Jews, 35,000 others; Jewish state about 55% Jewish. Land ownership (about 7% Jewish, unsourced) must be verified from Village Statistics 1945 or the UNSCOP report.
- **[DISPUTED]** The Jewish Agency accepted despite objecting to borders; the Arab Higher Committee and Arab League rejected it as against self-determination and the Charter; Irgun and Lehi opposed. Lobbying claims are contested. The UK abstained.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine (first 100k) [W]. **Resolution text not read** (UNISPAL, UN library blocked). **[2ND]** primary needed.

### 18. Deir Yassin, 9 April 1948
- Date: 9 April 1948 (start time morning or evening, sources differ).
- Location: Deir Yassin, near Jerusalem (Wikipedia coordinates 31°47'12"N 35°10'42"E - unverified). Category: conflict.
- **[DISPUTED]** Attack by the Irgun and Lehi (about 120-132 men) with Haganah/Palmach support. Death toll: 254 (Irgun's initial claim, repeated by the press); 117 (al-Aref, 1956); 101 (Tauber); 107 (Kanaana / Bir Zeit, 1988); about 100-110 (Morris); 93 massacred plus fighting deaths (Pappé); 110-140 (Hogan); Red Cross delegate de Reynier (memo 13 April, read) saw about 50 bodies in the village and about 20 outside, and was told 200 dead; a medical-aid doctor counted about 100. Population: about 400 (ICRC) to about 750 (Khalidi). Contested points: about 25 villagers executed in a quarry (Kanaana, Gelber), claims of 80 prisoners shot, rape and mutilation allegations. The Haganah and Jewish Agency condemned the attack; the Jewish Agency apologised to King Abdullah, who rejected it. Why the toll was exaggerated: Pappé (to induce flight); Begin ("legend" that helped Irgun); Hazem Nusseibeh (an error that fuelled flight). A reprisal on a Hadassah convoy on 13 April killed 78 [W].
- Read: https://www.israel-palestina.info/achtergrond/deir-yassin-the-red-cross-report/ (ICRC memo translation, annotated by Matthew Hogan, partisan site but primary text); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Yassin_massacre [W]. Morris and Khalidi JPS not read. Era note: date is before 14 May, so `british-mandate`.

### 19. Fall of Haifa and flight of its Arab population, 21-22 April 1948
- Date: Haganah offensive 21-22 April 1948 (Operation Bi'ur Hametz), after British forces withdrew from positions on 20-21 April [W].
- Location: Haifa. Category: displacement.
- Figures (Morris 2004, via [W]): pre-war population about 135,000-140,000 (Arabs about 65,000-70,000); 25,000-30,000 Arabs had left by mid-March; about 15,000 fled 21-22 April; about 4,000 Arabs remained by mid-May. Arab dead unknown (100-300 estimated).
- **[DISPUTED]** Morris: mortar fire aimed to break Arab morale; evacuation encouraged by local Arab leaders and endorsed by the Arab Higher Committee; the Haganah urged women, children and elderly to leave. Karsh: the AHC played a key role. Walid Khalidi ("The Fall of Haifa", 1959): terror and psychological warfare, no Arab orders to leave. Pappé: terror campaign. Appeals by Jewish leaders to stay were NOT found.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Haifa_(1948) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_the_1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight (partial) [W]. JPS and Morris not read. Overlaps with the existing sample 1948 entry in the data; coordinate with the next era.

### 20. End of the British Mandate and the Israeli Declaration of Independence, 14 May 1948
- Date: 14 May 1948 (5 Iyar 5708), Tel Aviv; British control ended at midnight; Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Iraq (and Lebanon per the infobox) entered on 15 May.
- Location: Tel Aviv (declaration); effect Palestine-wide, `approximate`. Category: political / conflict.
- Why: The Declaration cites the 29 Nov 1947 resolution and invites Arab inhabitants to take part in the state on the basis of equal citizenship; it did not fix borders (to verify). Outcome per [W]: Israel held about 78% of Mandate Palestine; over 750,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled; armistices in 1949.
- **[DISPUTED]** Arab League cable: restoring order, no legal authority after the Mandate, a unitary Palestinian state. Gelber: Arab states intervened after Palestinian collapse to avoid a flood of refugees; Shlaim and Rogan: Abdullah's contacts with the Jewish Agency and annexation aims; Plan Dalet: Khalidi and Pappé say a blueprint for conquest and expulsion, Morris says defensive. Kirkbride's recollection of an Azzam Pasha remark is anecdotal. The civil war phase began after 29 Nov 1947, so 15 May starts the interstate phase.
- Read: https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/israel.asp (text); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War (partial) [W]. The Arab League cable text was not found. Consider separate cards for the end of the Mandate and the Declaration.

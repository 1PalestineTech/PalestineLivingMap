## Candidates

### 1. Ottoman conquest of Greater Syria and Palestine, 1516-1517
- Date: Marj Dabiq 24 Aug 1516; Khan Yunis/Gaza 28 Oct 1516; Ridaniyya 24 Jan 1517 (one summary said 22 Jan). **[DATE]** day of Ridaniyya
- Location: Palestine-wide (Gaza/Khan Yunis battle); `approximate`
- Why: Ends Mamluk rule and starts four centuries of Ottoman rule; Palestine organised into the sanjaks of Gaza, Jerusalem, Nablus, Lajjun and Safed under Damascus.
- Category: political. Not seriously contested.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Mamluk_War_(1516%E2%80%931517); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Palestine
- Underlying: Faroqhi (2009); Daly & Petry (1998); Ze'evi (1996) p.2; Gerber (1998) pp.565-566. Britannica "Marj Dabiq" 403.

### 2. Suleiman rebuilds the walls of Jerusalem, c. 1537-1541
- Date: start 1535/1536/1537/1538 depending on source; end 1541. Use year range, `circa`. **[DATE]**
- Location: Jerusalem (Old City), `area`/`exact`
- Why: The walls and gates still define the Old City; commissioned as an Islamic prestige project (inscription).
- Category: cultural / religious-sites. Motive (response to Charles V's 1535 Tunis capture) is hedged ("apparently") - do not state as fact. Architect attribution to Sinan unproven.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Jerusalem; https://cojs.org/?p=7394
- Underlying: Murphy-O'Connor, Oxford Archaeological Guide to the Holy Land (2008) p.12; Nathan Schur (1992).

### 3. The 16th-century Ottoman tax registers and Palestine's population
- Date: 1530s-1590s (registers e.g. 1596-97); `century`/range **[DATE]**
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`
- Why: The defters are the main source for early Ottoman population and religious composition; Muslims were the large majority.
- Category: demographic. **[DISPUTED]** figures differ by scholar and multiplier: Sergio DellaPergola (2001): ~156,000 (1533-39), ~205,000 (1553-54); Ze'evi (1996): ~200,000; Bernard Lewis: ~300,000. Method (household multiplier, undercount of bachelors) is debated. Numbers rule applies: name source and date for every figure.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Palestine_(region); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Palestine (secondary only)
- Underlying (NOT opened): Hütteroth & Abdulfattah, Historical Geography of Palestine, Transjordan and Southern Syria in the Late 16th Century (Palm & Enke, 1977); Lewis & Cohen, Population and Revenue in the Towns of Palestine in the Sixteenth Century (Princeton, 1978). **No primary figures retrieved - weakest-sourced item.**

### 4. Safed becomes a centre of Jewish learning; Hebrew press set up, c. 1577
- Date: Karo in Safed from c.1535, Shulchan Arukh completed 1555, printed 1565, Karo died 24 Mar 1575; Safed press 1577-80 (first book/month unconfirmed). **[DATE]**
- Location: Safed (Tzfat), `area`
- Why: After the 1492 Sephardi influx Safed became a major Jewish scholarly and mystical centre (Karo, Luria, Cordovero); its press is cited as the first in the region (check wording).
- Category: cultural / religious. Context (separate possible event): Joseph Nasi and Tiberias grant, c.1558-65; sources differ on whether Suleiman or Selim II granted it.
- Read: https://cojs.org/?p=36593; https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/joseph-nasi; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Karo
- Not contested. 2ND not needed.

### 5. Fakhr al-Din II extends Druze emirate into Galilee, c. 1602-1635
- Date: sanjak-bey of Safed from 1602; exile 1613-1618; Gaza sanjak grant 1623 largely blocked; executed in Istanbul March/April 1635 (13 Apr per infobox). **[DATE]** capture year (1633/1634), exact execution date
- Location: Galilee (Safed/Acre/Tiberias), `approximate`
- Why: Shows a powerful local emir bargaining with Istanbul; control of Galilee documented, control of wider Palestine contested/unrealised.
- Category: political. Interpretation (Lebanese-nationalist vs Ottoman-provincial) not found in pages read. **[2ND]**
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhr_al-Din_II (first ~100k of 194k chars)
- Underlying: Abu-Husayn (1985); Hourani (2010); Harris (2012). Britannica 403.

### 6. Daher al-Umar's autonomous rule centred on Acre, c. 1730-1775
- Date: Tiberias c.1730; Acre taken 1744, formal tax-farm 1746 (sources differ); killed 21 or 22 Aug 1775; Jazzar takes Acre 19 Sep 1775. **[DATE]**
- Location: Acre (Akka), Galilee
- Why: Largely autonomous Galilee domain tied to the cotton trade with French merchants; ended by an Ottoman naval siege.
- Category: political / economy-daily-life. Interpretation (local autonomy vs rebel vs proto-state) varies by historian; details of his death disputed (Stefan Winter treats one account as legend).
- Read: https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/zahir-al-umars-stronghold-galilee; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daher_al-Umar (partial)
- Underlying: Thomas Philipp, Acre: The Rise and Fall of a Palestinian City 1730-1831; Joudah, Revolt in Palestine in the Eighteenth Century; Amnon Cohen, Palestine in the 18th Century. Britannica 403.

### 7. Napoleon's campaign: Jaffa taken and Acre besieged, March-May 1799
- Date: Jaffa siege 3 Mar, taken by 7 Mar 1799; Acre 20 Mar-21 May 1799 (final assault failed 10 May).
- Location: Jaffa; Acre
- Why: First modern European invasion of Palestine; failed at Acre, defended by Jazzar Pasha with British naval support (Sidney Smith).
- Category: conflict. **[DISPUTED]** killing of Jaffa prisoners: counts range ~2,000-4,000+ (2,100 / 2,440 / 4,100 in different places); justification (parole broken, no food/escort, plague risk) contested. Only a French/European-centred account was read. **[2ND]** need Ottoman/Arab or French primary accounts (Bourrienne, Desgenettes, Miot, Napoleon's memoirs named in literature but unread).
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jaffa; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Acre_(1799); Wikisource Britannica 9th ed. "Acre"

### 8. Egyptian conquest under Ibrahim Pasha, 1831-1832 (rule to 1840)
- Date: campaign opens Oct-Nov 1831; Acre stormed 27 May 1832; Convention of Kutahya 1833; Egyptian rule ends 1840-41. Siege start date varies by source. **[DATE]**
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate` (Acre)
- Why: Ten years of Egyptian rule with conscription, disarmament and new taxes; context for the 1834 revolt and for expanded European consular presence.
- Category: political / conflict. Dates agreed; interpretation (modernising vs harsh) differs.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian%E2%80%93Ottoman_War_(1831%E2%80%931833)
- Underlying: Fahmy, All the Pasha's Men (2002); Aksan (2007); Dodwell (1931).

### 9. The 1834 Peasants' Revolt
- Date: conscription order 25 Apr 1834; rebels enter Jerusalem about 20-21 May; Ibrahim re-enters 30 May; Nablus 15 Jul; Hebron 4 Aug; ends Aug 1834. Siege end date (7 Jun?) conflicts. **[DATE]**
- Location: Jerusalem; Nablus region; Hebron
- Why: Major uprising against conscription and disarmament; its meaning for Palestinian identity is debated.
- Category: conflict. **[DISPUTED]** Kimmerling & Migdal: helped shape Palestinian identity; Benny Morris: rebels sought restoration of Ottoman rule, not independence; Adel Manna on how historiography shaped memory; Asad Rustum's account largely Egyptian-sourced. Figures (e.g. ~10,000 deported, ~500 killed in Hebron) are Wikipedia-only, unverified. Do not use claims of violence against Hebron's Jews without a read source.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peasants%27_revolt_in_Palestine (partial). JPS article "The Time the Peasants Entered Jerusalem" exists but 403.
- Underlying: Kimmerling & Migdal, Palestinians: The Making of a People (2003); Manna (2009); Rood (2004); Heacock (2008).

### 10. Status Quo of the Christian holy places, firmans of 1757 and 1852/53
- Date: 1757 firman (Osman III); 1852 and 1853 firmans (Abdulmecid I) - year only; 1856 Treaty of Paris Art. 9. **[DATE]** no day/month found; 1757 origin story weakly sourced
- Location: Church of the Holy Sepulchre, Jerusalem; Church of the Nativity, Bethlehem
- Why: Froze the rights of the Christian communities in the shrines; still in force; entangled with the lead-up to the Crimean War.
- Category: religious-sites. Interpretations of the Status Quo and of the firmans' role in the Crimean War are debated; scholarly source not read. **[2ND]**
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_Quo_(Jerusalem_and_Bethlehem)

### 11. Ottoman Land Code, 1858
- Date: 1858 (year only; some sources date first Palestine tapu registries to 1859 - unverified). **[DATE]**
- Location: Palestine-wide, `approximate`
- Why: Required registration of land and title deeds; separated registered owner from cultivator; central to later land-ownership debates.
- Category: land-settlement. **[DISPUTED]** Older view (Waldner): peasants avoided registration, lost land to notables; newer view: outcomes varied, hill-country peasants did register, large estates mostly from state wasteland sales; Ruth Kark: land concentrated in absentee effendi families, later easing purchases by Jews, Germans and others; Munir Fakher Eldin (Birzeit): contested legacies. Alff argues 1858 was not a legal turning point (unread).
- Read: https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/land-code-1858; https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/consequences-of-the-ottoman-land-law-agrarian-and-privatization-p/; https://www.eume-berlin.de/en/news-press/news/details/conflicting-legacies-of-the-1858-ottoman-land-law-a-view-from-palestine

### 12. German Templer colonies begin, Haifa 1868 and Jaffa 1869
- Date: Hoffmann and Hardegg land at Haifa 30 Oct 1868; Jaffa 1869; Sarona 1871 or 1872 (source conflicts). **[DATE]**
- Location: Haifa; Jaffa; Sarona
- Why: Planned European agricultural/urban colonies that introduced new building, farming and transport technology, and left a distinct architectural legacy.
- Category: land-settlement / economy-daily-life. Founding not contested; later history (WWII) is a separate event.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Templer_colonies_in_Palestine (only source; weak)
- Underlying: Ruhe, Door of Hope (1983); Goldman, 2003 Haifa PhD thesis.

### 13. Jerusalem made a mutasarrifate answering directly to Istanbul, 1872
- Date: 1872 (Wikipedia); direct-to-Istanbul arrangement from 1841 not formal provincial status. Alternative 1874 / 1887 dates **not supported** by anything read. **[DATE]**
- Location: Jerusalem (district: Jerusalem, Jaffa, Hebron, Gaza, Beersheba; Nazareth added 1906)
- Why: Gave Jerusalem and its notables a distinct administrative status, relevant to later diplomacy and to the notable families.
- Category: political. Not contested.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarrifate_of_Jerusalem; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayet_of_Beirut
- Underlying: Abu-Manneh (1999); Büssow, Hamidian Palestine (2011); Kushner (1987).

### 14. First Aliyah and the first Zionist agricultural settlements, 1882
- Date: Biluim reach Jaffa 6 Jul 1882 (JVL); Petah Tikva founded 1878, abandoned (malaria), re-settled 1883; Rishon LeZion 31 Jul 1882 and Zikhron Ya'akov 1882 are unverified snippet-level. **[DATE]**
- Location: Jaffa area; Petah Tikva; Rishon LeZion; Zikhron Ya'akov
- Why: Start of organised Jewish agricultural settlement, within Ottoman restrictions on immigration and land purchase (1881/82 onwards), and with local Arab reactions (e.g. March 1886 clash at Petah Tikva, motives not given by the source read).
- Category: land-settlement / demographic. **[DISPUTED]** Zionist-historiography framing: legal, funded land purchase hindered by Ottoman limits; critical framing: purchases from large landlords with tenant eviction (Pappé), "maximum land, minimum Arabs" (Masalha), displaced numbers possibly higher than recorded (Porath). Kark: effendi sales formed the core. **[2ND]** need a primary or neutral academic source (Mandel; Kark) read directly.
- Read: https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/the-first-aliyah-1882-1903; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petah_Tikva; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_land_purchase_in_Palestine (flagged disputed); https://cojs.org/?p=36670 (1893 British FO draft re land-sale bar)

### 15. Jaffa-Jerusalem railway opens, 26 September 1892
- Date: official opening 26 Sep 1892 (two pages agree); first train reached Jerusalem 21 Aug 1892; Jaffa-Ramla section 24 May 1891 (milestones, not disagreement).
- Location: Jaffa station to Jerusalem; ~86.5 km
- Why: Cut travel time from about a day or ten hours to a few hours, boosting pilgrimage, trade, tourism and Jaffa's port economy.
- Category: economy-daily-life. Cost not resolved (14 million francs vs "£14 million") - omit. Population-doubling claim is Wikipedia-only, unchecked.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa%E2%80%93Jerusalem_railway (partial); https://www.firststation.co.il/en/the-history-of-the-first-station/ (tourism site)
- Underlying: Travis (2009); Cotterell (1984); Kark (1990).

### 17. Yusuf Diya al-Khalidi's letter to Zadok Kahn for Herzl, 1 March 1899
- Date: 1 March 1899; Herzl replied 19 March 1899 (both pages agree).
- Location: Jerusalem (sent to Paris)
- Why: A frank early warning from a Jerusalem notable and former mayor about Zionism's effects; Herzl's reply defended the project's benefits to the empire and local population.
- Category: political. Reading of Herzl's reply differs (Vridar reads it as sidestepping the objection; no Zionist-historiography source read). **[2ND]** Khalidi's life dates differ (1842-1906 per Wikipedia, "citation needed"; 1829-1907 per PASSIA). 
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_al-Khalidi; https://vridar.org/2020/11/21/in-the-name-of-god-let-palestine-be-left-alone (blog - pointer only, do not cite)
- Underlying: Rashid Khalidi, The Hundred Years' War on Palestine (2020); Büssow (2011); Scholch, Jerusalem Quarterly (2005).

### 18. After the 1908 Young Turk revolution: parliament and the Haifa paper al-Karmil
- Date: al-Karmil first issue Dec 1908 (Najib Nassar, Haifa); Ruhi al-Khalidi elected 1908 as a Jerusalem deputy; first major Zionism debate in parliament March-May 1911 (unopened snippet). Revolution date and election details not read. **[DATE]**
- Location: Haifa (al-Karmil); Jerusalem (deputies)
- Why: Press and parliamentary politics opened up; al-Karmil campaigned against Zionist settlement, and deputies Ruhi al-Khalidi and Said al-Husayni raised the issue in Istanbul.
- Category: political / cultural. Consider splitting into two events (al-Karmil; 1911 parliamentary debate). Khalidi said he opposed Jewish migration, not the residence of local Jews.
- Read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karmil_(newspaper); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhi_Khalidi
- Underlying: Khalidi, Palestinian Identity; Muslih (1989); Beska (2011/2014/2016). Britannica 403.

### 19. Falastin newspaper founded in Jaffa, 1911

- Why: Became one of the most influential Arabic papers of Palestine, addressing readers as Palestinians and critical of Zionism; run by cousins Issa and Yusuf El-Issa; closed 1948, relaunched 1950.

### 20. First World War home front, 1915-1917: locusts, famine, evacuations, and the surrender of Jerusalem
- Date: locust plague Mar-Oct 1915; Gaza evacuated early March 1917; Jaffa evacuation ordered 28 Mar and carried out 6 Apr 1917; Jerusalem surrendered 9 Dec 1917, Allenby entered on foot 11 Dec. **[DATE]** sequence at Jerusalem varies
- Location: Palestine-wide; Jaffa; Gaza; Jerusalem
- Why: Hardship under Cemal Pasha and the end of Ottoman rule. Could be split into 2-3 events (locusts/famine; 1917 Jaffa and Gaza evacuations; surrender of Jerusalem).
- Category: demographic / displacement / political. **[DISPUTED]** Jaffa deportation: ~10,000 displaced (8,000 Jewish) per one article; ~1,500 deportees died (Haaretz 2014) vs burial records totalling 739 (Shragai); Friedman (1971) says Jews treated worse due to Cemal's anti-Zionism, Sheffy says cultural/behavioural factors. Gaza: 35,000-40,000 (Halevy and Sheffy). Locust impact: Zachary Foster, Middle Eastern Studies (2015) estimates wheat/barley harvests fell 10-15%, fruit and vegetables 60-100%. The "about half of Mount Lebanon died" figure is uncited and applies to Lebanon, **not Palestine; no Palestine-specific mortality figure found.** Mayor Hussein al-Husseini's role and death year (1918 or 1919) unsettled.
- Read: Wikipedia 1917 Jaffa deportation; Wikipedia 1915 Ottoman Syria locust plague; https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/general-edmond-allenby-marches-into-jerusalem (JVL, cites Sebag-Montefiore); Wikipedia Hussein al-Husayni
- Underlying: Foster (2015); Sebag-Montefiore, Jerusalem: The Biography; Benvenisti, City of Stone (1998) p.118; Wichhart, Jerusalem Quarterly 56-57 (unread).

## Additions from the PalQuest overview (pasted by the owner, 2026-11-03)

Source: PalQuest, "I. Ottoman Rule", by Alex Winder (https://www.palquest.org/en/highlight/31097/i-ottoman-rule).
Read as pasted text, not fetched (the site blocks automated access). PalQuest is a Palestinian-curated archive and its overview
uses interpretive framing (for example on Zionism and colonial expansion), so attribute those points rather than stating them as fact.

What it adds or changes:
- **#2 (walls):** it dates the Dome of the Rock renovation to 1537-1540, alongside the wall rebuilding. Could be a separate event or part of #2.
- **#3 / new demographic event:** Ottoman records give 462,465 people in 1878 across the sanjaks of Jerusalem, Nablus and Acre: 403,795 Muslims, 43,659 Christians, 15,011 Jews (per PalQuest, citing Ottoman records). It excludes about 10,000 foreign-citizen Jews, several thousand Bedouin and foreign Christians. This is a much better-anchored number than the 16th-century estimates and could replace #3 as the demographic anchor. Still needs the original (Ottoman census / Schölch, Palestine in Transformation).
- **#13 (administration):** PalQuest says that after the 1870s-80s reorganisations the area was split into the Sanjak of Jerusalem (direct to Istanbul) and the Sanjaks of Nablus and Acre (in Beirut province). Consistent with 1872; supports describing the whole arrangement.
- **#14 / #16:** says the first Zionist colony was 1878, first immigrant wave 1882, about 30 colonies and about 60,000 Jews (more than half recent immigrants) by 1914, and that most Jews of the period lived in Jerusalem, Hebron, Safed and Tiberias. Also notes Jewish immigration was fostered through the capitulations despite local opposition (PalQuest's view; **[2ND]** still needed).
- **#17:** confirms al-Khalidi sat in the 1876 Ottoman Parliament for Jerusalem (First Constitutional Era, suspended after about two years).
- **#18:** says deputies from Jerusalem, Jaffa, Nablus and Acre were elected to parliament in 1908, 1912 and 1914.
